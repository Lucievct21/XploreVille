<strong>Stop 1 - Porta San Paolo</strong>
We begin right here, at the southern entrance to the ancient city.
In front of you is the Porta San Paolo, one of the monumental gates of the Aurelian Walls, built in the 3rd century to protect Rome from external threats.
This gate was once called Porta Ostiensis, because it opened the road to Ostia, the great ancient port where goods from across the Mediterranean arrived.
Later, it became known as Porta San Paolo, since it also leads to the Basilica of Saint Paul Outside the Walls.
Look at the two towers flanking the passage: their solid masonry base was built to withstand all kinds of attacks.
This gate has witnessed many chapters of Rome’s history. One of the most striking is also one of the most recent: on September 10, 1943, just after Italy signed the armistice, German troops reached the gates of Rome.
Here, Italian soldiers and Roman civilians tried to defend the city. It was a desperate fight. By the end of the day, Rome had fallen.
But the Porta San Paolo still stands.
Let’s move on and enter inside the walls.
