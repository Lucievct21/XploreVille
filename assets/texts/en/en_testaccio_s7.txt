<strong>Stop 7 - Piazza dell'Emporio</strong>
Here we are on Piazza dell’Emporio, right on the southern edge of Testaccio, next to the Tiber River.
Just below us, on the right bank, once stood the Ripa Grande port, one of the largest river ports in medieval Rome. It was completely demolished in the 19th century when the embankments were built to control the river’s floods.
Across the water, on the left bank, are the remains of the ancient Portus Tiberinus, Rome’s first commercial port, dating back to the 2nd century BC.
And if you look across the river, you’ll spot the neighborhood of Trastevere. The bridge you see here, Ponte dell’Industria, was built in 1918 to connect Trastevere with the newly expanding railway lines.
Now, turn around and look up: that’s the Aventine Hill, one of Rome’s famous seven hills. The quiet neighborhood built on it is now one of the city’s most elegant. The bell tower you see belongs to the Abbey of Saint Anselm, which today also houses a pontifical university.
We’re now heading onto Via Marmorata, or “Marble Street.” It gets its name from the huge blocks of marble that used to be unloaded here from river ports and stacked along the road, waiting to be sold.
