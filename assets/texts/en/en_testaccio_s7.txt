<strong>Stop 7 - Piazza dell'Emporio</strong>
Here we are on Piazza dell’Emporio, on the southern edge of Testaccio, right next to the Tiber River.
Just below us, on the right bank, once stood the Port of Ripa Grande, the main river port of Rome during the Middle Ages and the early modern period. It was used both for unloading goods coming up the Tiber and for activities related to the maintenance of boats. The port disappeared around 1880, following the construction of the embankment walls designed to control the river’s floods. .
Directly opposite, on the left bank, are the remains of the Emporium, the ancient river port and commercial hub of Roman antiquity, active from the 2nd century BC. This is where goods were unloaded in amphorae, which later formed what is now Mount Testaccio. 
If you now look across the river, you can see the neighborhood of Trastevere. The bridge that connects us to it is the Ponte Sublicio, whose name comes from the oldest bridge in Rome, entirely made of wood, which once stood roughly in the same location in antiquity. 
Now turn around: behind you rises the Aventine Hill, one of Rome’s seven historic hills. The neighborhood that developed here is quiet, residential… and highly sought after. The bell tower you see belongs to the Abbey of Saint Anselm, which today also houses a pontifical university.
We’re now heading onto Via Marmorata, literally “Marble Street.” It gets its name from the huge blocks of marble that used to be unloaded here from the river ports and stacked along the road, waiting to be sold.
