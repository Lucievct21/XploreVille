<strong>Stop 3 - Non-Catholic Cemetery</strong>
Just behind the pyramid lies a peaceful, somewhat hidden spot…
This is Rome’s Non-Catholic Cemetery, also known as the English Cemetery.
It’s a place full of emotion, almost suspended in time.
The cemetery was founded in the early 18th century, back when the Papal States still ruled the city. At the time, non-Catholics weren’t allowed to be buried in Roman cemeteries so burials took place in secret, often at night, in this then-rural area, on the edge of the city.
Little by little, the Holy See began to tolerate these burials, and eventually a wall was built… but that happened more than a century later.
Today, the cemetery is the resting place of Protestants, Orthodox Christians, Jews, Muslims, and secular people… individuals from all over the world, with very different life stories.
Among the most famous are the English poets John Keats and Percy Shelley, the philosopher and founder of the Italian Communist Party Antonio Gramsci, and Giorgio Napolitano, former President of the Italian Republic.
It’s also a very photogenic spot: cypress-lined paths, the pyramid in the background, ancient Roman walls all around… and a few cats wandering peacefully among the graves.
If you have a bit of time, step inside. Entry is free, though a €5 donation is suggested.
