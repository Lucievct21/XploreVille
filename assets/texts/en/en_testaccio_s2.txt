<strong>Stop 2 - Pyramid of Cestius</strong>
No, you’re not dreaming: a pyramid, right in the heart of Rome.
This is the Pyramid of Cestius, a Roman tomb built between 18 and 12 BCE.
At that time, Egypt had just been conquered by Rome, and Roman elites were fascinated by its culture. Obelisks, exotic paintings… Egyptian style was all the rage.
Gaius Cestius, an ambitious politician, decided to go big: he asked to be buried in a pyramid.
The result? A monument 36 meters high, built in just 330 days, using Roman concrete and covered entirely in Carrara marble.
Inside, there’s a tiny funerary chamber measuring only 6 by 4 meters which represents less than 1% of the total volume.
Today, a hole in the stone still exists: it dates back to the Middle Ages, when thieves came looking for a treasure… but left empty-handed.
