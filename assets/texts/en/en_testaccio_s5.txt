<strong>Stop 5 - Mattatoio</strong>
After Mount Testaccio, we head to a landmark of the neighborhood’s working-class history: the old Testaccio slaughterhouse, known as il Mattatoio.
Built in 1888, during the time when Testaccio was growing into an industrial district, it was designed to modernize and streamline the meat production process. The site featured large brick structures, divided into specialized pavilions, all built to meet hygiene standards that were remarkably strict for the time.
The slaughterhouse operated until the 1970s, eventually closing in 1975 when a new facility was built on the outskirts of Rome.
For a while, the complex was abandoned and slowly fell into disrepair.
But in the early 2000s, il Mattatoio was given a new lease on life through major rehabilitation projects. Today, it’s a vibrant cultural and artistic hub: it hosts temporary exhibitions, part of the architecture faculty, a music school, and local community organizations. The vast open spaces are also used for concerts, festivals, and markets, making the site lively and open to all once again.
The Mattatoio also left a deep mark on the local food culture. The workers passed down humble, traditional recipes based on the quinto quarto, literally the “fifth quarter,” referring to the less noble cuts of meat, like offal. These culinary traditions are still alive today in the osterie of Testaccio, which serve up some of the most authentic Roman dishes.
A way of keeping this place’s story going - one plate at a time.
